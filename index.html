How the model works, and where it stops

Stressed volume = max(0, total volume − unstressed capacity). Pmsf = stressed volume / venous compliance. Venous return = max(0, (Pmsf − RAP) / RVR). Cardiac function is a simplified rising curve with a maximum output: min(pump capacity, contractility × max(0, RAP − external pressure)). Where the two curves cross sets RAP and CO. The model doesn't simulate pulsatile chamber pressures. When fluid, blood or relief of an obstruction brings flow back, HR, arteriolar tone and venoconstriction ease back toward baseline, the way the baroreflex would. This doesn't reverse vasoplegia.

CO = HR × SV / 1,000. MAP = CO × SVR / 80 + RAP. CaO₂ = 1.34 × Hb × SaO₂ + 0.003 × PaO₂ (saturation as a fraction). DO₂ = CO × CaO₂ × 10. VTI = SV / 3.14 for a 2.0 cm LVOT. Estimated ScvO₂ = SaO₂ − VO₂ / (1.34 × Hb × CO × 10). Central and mixed venous saturations are not interchangeable. The ScvO₂ readout is a simplified estimate; it doesn't account for upper-body extraction being different from the rest of the body. The new mixed venous SvO₂ uses Fick-derived venous content including dissolved venous oxygen, with a fixed Hill approximation (P50 26.8 mmHg, exponent 2.7).

Swan model: PCWP = external pressure + left/right filling factor × max(0, RAP − external pressure). The factor is set for each phenotype to show how congested the left side is (baseline 1.6, LV failure 2.4, PE 0.4). There's no fixed relationship between CVP and PCWP in real patients. The pump model is the same as before; PVR adds RV afterload and flattens the RV's curve. PCWP is estimated from the rest of the model. It doesn't come from a separate LV pressure-volume loop.

Mean PAP = PCWP + CO × PVR (Wood units). PA pulse pressure ≈ SV / pulmonary arterial compliance, capped so PAD stays above PCWP. PAS = mean PAP + ⅔ pulse pressure; PAD = mean PAP − ⅓ pulse pressure. RV systolic ≈ PAS and RV end-diastolic ≈ RAP, assuming no pulmonic valve gradient. These are approximate pressures; the model doesn't draw waveforms. Pulmonary edema worsens as transmural left-sided filling pressure rises, and edema that's already there can take a while to clear after unloading.

CI = CO / BSA; SVI = SV / BSA; PVR (dyn·s/cm⁵) = 80 × (mean PAP − PCWP) / CO; SVRI = SVR × BSA; PVRI = PVR × BSA. TPG = mean PAP − PCWP; DPG = PAD − PCWP; PAPi = (PAS − PAD) / RAP. CPO = MAP × CO / 451; CPI = CPO / BSA. LVSWI = 0.0136 × (MAP − PCWP) × SVI; RVSWI = 0.0136 × (mean PAP − RAP) × SVI. MAP and HR come from systemic monitoring, not the PA distal port.

PCWP approximates mean LA pressure when it's measured correctly and there's no pulmonary vein or mitral disease. It isn't always the same as LVEDP. External pressure can raise the measured PCWP without filling the LA any more. In a real patient, LA size also depends on compliance, rhythm and chronic remodeling, so a high PCWP doesn't need a big LA. The model doesn't estimate LA volume, RV ejection fraction or RV end-diastolic volume. The two RV numbers need a volumetric catheter and more assumptions.

Demand and consumption are different things. Consumption is capped by how much oxygen the tissue can extract, so it can fall below demand. Shunting lowers effective extraction, which is how venous saturation stays high while the tissue runs up an oxygen debt. The lactate and CO₂ gap values are set by hand for each phenotype; no equation produces them. B-lines and IVC findings are simplified for teaching. The IVC alone doesn't tell you volume status or fluid responsiveness.

The critical curve uses assumed BSA 1.8 m² and an illustrative DO₂I knee of 330 mL/min/m². Treat the curve as a reference. It isn't a universal threshold, and the phenotypes don't all follow it exactly. Extraction zones: normal around 25%, compensation around 35–50%, severe extraction above 60%; extraction failure can cause shock at a low global O₂ER.

Baseline anchors: Hb 15, SaO₂ 98%, PaO₂ 95, HR 70, SV 71, CO approximately 5, CaO₂ approximately 20, DO₂ approximately 1,000, VO₂ 250, O₂ER approximately 25%, SvO₂ approximately 73%, SVR 1,200, RAP 5, MAP approximately 80, Pmsf 7.5. Computed VTI is 22.6 cm (a common teaching approximation is 20). The reservoir narrows with venoconstriction and widens with venodilation. Width tracks unstressed capacity and compliance. The area of blood stays proportional to volume, so a change in tone doesn't add or remove blood. With constriction the unstressed layer shrinks and the stressed layer grows, and the divider also moves because the width changed. The 70/30 split between unstressed and stressed volume is a teaching approximation of venous capacitance.

In the case, the observed numbers sit in their own box, apart from the model's estimates. The model can't hit every observed value without breaking its own equations. BP 82/40 gives a calculated MAP of 54. The treatment stage and the one-hour values follow the case; animation time isn't clinical time. The case isn't a treatment order for real patients.

Each drug button shows one mechanism. The real drugs (norepinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, dobutamine, epinephrine and milrinone) each have their own vascular, chronotropic and metabolic effects. The sedation, ventilation and cooling button only lowers demand; in a real patient, induction and positive-pressure ventilation can also drop venous return. The oxygen button counts any rise in saturation. Going from a PaO₂ of 100 to 500 mmHg adds only 1.2 mL/dL, all of it dissolved. Early in acute hemorrhage the Hb may not have dropped yet; this preset shows hemorrhage after the blood has diluted and equilibrated.
