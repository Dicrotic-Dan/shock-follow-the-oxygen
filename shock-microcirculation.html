How this close-up works

This is a companion teaching model, not a second monitor. Delivery and demand use normalized oxygen units per minute (normal delivery 100, demand 25). They are not mL/min, clinical capillary velocities, or blood saturations. When you open it with Zoom, it takes flow, arterial oxygen content, demand, edema and a matching tissue pattern from the whole-circulation page. Regional extraction is illustrative and won't exactly match global uptake. Anything you change here stays here; Zoom out takes you back to the same patient and interventions.

Total delivery = flow × arterial oxygen content. Three parallel nutritive pathways share the non-shunted flow. Flow heterogeneity redistributes delivery unevenly among them; each supplies one third of tissue demand. For each region, uptake = the smaller of local demand and local delivered oxygen × extraction capacity. Edema lowers illustrative extraction capacity by increasing diffusion distance. Venous oxygen remaining = delivery − uptake; mixed venous content relative to arterial content = 100 × (1 − uptake / delivery). This is a content ratio, not SvO₂.

Unmet demand = demand − actual uptake. When supply is inadequate, uptake falls even though demand persists. Cells are colored by local demand met; the global extraction ratio can remain low in extraction failure. Gold-dot density follows actual uptake. Bypass blood returns without unloading. Microvascular shunting is drawn as a separate route for clarity; real sepsis can involve heterogeneous capillary transit and impaired extraction without a discrete anatomical bypass.

The simplified model does not calculate tissue PO₂, ATP concentration, lactate, pH, or mitochondrial function. Real oxygen diffusion also depends on pressure gradients, capillary recruitment, hemoglobin dissociation, and cellular metabolism. Edema and reduced extraction are illustrative rather than a validated quantitative diffusion model.
